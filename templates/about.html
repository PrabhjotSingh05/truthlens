TruthLens

Credibility Engine · Built to fight misinformation

Overview

TruthLens is a free, AI-powered credibility analysis tool that helps you evaluate the reliability of news articles and images before you share them. In an era of viral misinformation, deepfakes, and manipulated media, TruthLens provides a fast, independent second opinion — backed by multiple layers of automated analysis.

When you submit a URL, TruthLens fetches the article, extracts its content, and runs it through a multi-stage analysis pipeline: it checks the publisher's historical reputation, scores the writing for objectivity and sensationalism, detects logical fallacies and propaganda techniques, cross-references the story against other news sources, and produces a plain-language credibility report. Image submissions undergo a parallel visual forensics process that detects manipulation artifacts, extracts embedded text, identifies people, and fact-checks any claims the image makes against live web sources.

TruthLens is designed for general audiences — no technical knowledge is needed. All you need is a URL or an image file. Results are displayed in seconds and are free to use for personal, educational, or journalistic research purposes.

How It Works
1

Content Fetching & Extraction

TruthLens fetches the submitted URL and extracts the article's text, metadata, author information, publication date, and social media links. For images, it reads pixel data and embedded metadata directly from the uploaded file.

2

Heuristic Pre-Screening

Before any AI call, a rules-based scanner checks for red flags: missing HTTPS, absent contact/privacy pages, suspicious domain patterns, excessive capital letters, and emotional trigger words commonly found in clickbait.

3

Multi-Layer AI Analysis

Two independent AI analysis passes examine the content from different angles — one focused on deep credibility scoring (factual grounding, objectivity, transparency, bias, propaganda indicators), the other on cross-referencing claims against related news coverage. The results are then synthesised into a single weighted verdict.

4

Live Web Cross-Reference

For both articles and images, TruthLens searches Google News in real time to find whether the same story is being reported by other outlets, whether the claims are corroborated or contradicted, and whether an image is an old photo being reused out of context.

5

Credibility Report

All signals are compiled into a structured report: trust scores, claim verdict, political bias, sentiment, narrative technique detection, key findings, related sources, and a plain-English recommendation. For images, additional forensic notes, time-period estimation, and manipulation artifact lists are included.

Limitations

TruthLens is a research and awareness tool. It is not infallible. Understanding its limitations helps you use it effectively:

  • AI analysis can be wrong. Language models can misread context, miss satire, or misjudge unfamiliar writing styles. Always treat results as a starting point, not a final verdict.
  • New or obscure publishers score lower. Sites with little historical presence receive lower trust scores by default, even if the individual article is accurate.
  • Paywalled or JavaScript-heavy sites may not extract correctly. If article text cannot be fetched, the analysis falls back to title and metadata only.
  • Image forensics is probabilistic. Sophisticated AI-generated images may pass visual checks. Authentic images with unusual editing (filters, crops) may be flagged unnecessarily.
  • Not a replacement for journalism. TruthLens does not replace professional fact-checking organisations. For high-stakes decisions, consult multiple independent sources.
